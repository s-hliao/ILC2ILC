ILC2Real: Sample-Efficient Policy Learning and Adaptation with Iterative Learning Control

Abstract

Transferring robot policies from simulation to hardware has proven to be a challenging task. Learning techniques typically address this problem with domain randomization: training in simulation against as wide a range of dynamics as possible, in the hope that the real system falls within it. This assumes the randomized family covers deployment, and it is sample-inefficient. We present an orthogonal approach, ILC2Real, which instead assumes only that the simulator's sensitivities point roughly in the right direction, and corrects the policy with real dynamics. ILC2Real learns in a nominal simulator and fine-tunes to deployment dynamics with a small number of guided improvements, both stages using Iterative Learning Control (ILC). Classical ILC uses approximate Jacobians to converge to locally optimal feedforward inputs for a single trajectory, and does not generalize beyond it. ILC2Real instead makes the ILC update the learning rule of a goal-conditioned neural policy. In simulation, every trial takes an ILC step and the network regresses onto a replay buffer of corrected actions. On hardware, the same update uses measured errors to correct the network across all goals from trials at a few, with no adaptation module, and no randomization required, though the method is compatible with both. On a quadruped jumping task, we show that ILC2Real trains with 40 to 53 times fewer samples and 10 to 30 times less time in simulation than domain-randomization baselines (PPO, RMA, FADA), and after 24 real trials reaches 1.6 to 1.9 times their success rate in sim-to-sim transfer.
